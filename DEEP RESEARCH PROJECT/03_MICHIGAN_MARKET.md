# PART 3 — METRO DETROIT MARKET

## Verdict: **C — oversaturated at the low end, thinly served at the top end.**

Not "underserved." Anyone telling you Metro Detroit needs another used-appliance dealer is
selling you a truckload.

## Competitive density (verified counts from S&D directory aggregators)

| Market | S&D / used appliance outlets listed |
|---|---:|
| Detroit | **15** |
| Warren | 4 |
| Livonia | 3 (one directory), 2 (another) |
| Flint | 7 |
| Pontiac | 5 |
| Michigan statewide | 24 (conservative directory) to **374 across 160 cities** (aggressive directory) |

Plus **ABC Warehouse** — a Pontiac-headquartered chain with used and scratch-and-dent lines,
a real balance sheet, existing delivery infrastructure, and brand recognition you cannot buy.

Plus the channel that actually sets your price ceiling: **Facebook Marketplace and
Craigslist.** Verified Detroit-metro listings include an LG gas dryer at $345, an LG washer at
$250, a Frigidaire refrigerator at $200, and an LG washer/dryer set at $625. Every one of
those competes directly with a scratch-and-dent unit you paid $165 landed for and want $385
for.

**Detroit's used-appliance price floor is the lowest of any market in the competitor set
(Part 2).** That is a demand-side consequence of the region's income profile, and it is
structural. It compresses the price you can charge without compressing what you pay for
inventory — the worst combination.

## Market size, bottom-up

Wayne + Oakland + Macomb ≈ **2.1M households**. At roughly **0.4 major-appliance purchases per
household per year** (four appliances replaced on a ~10-year cycle), that is **~840,000 units
per year** region-wide across all price points and all channels.

The secondary market — used, refurbished, scratch-and-dent — is realistically **~15%** of that:
**~126,000 units/year, ~10,500 units/month, spread across 25–35 dealers, ABC Warehouse, and
thousands of private Marketplace sellers.**

**A dominant new independent might capture 6–8% of the secondary market: 630–840 units per
month.** That is the absolute ceiling used in the $1M feasibility solver. It is also roughly
four times the volume at which my model's profit peaks, which tells you the binding constraint
is not demand — it is your cost structure.

**[ESTIMATE]** — the 0.4/household and 15% secondary-share figures are my derivations, not
published statistics. They are the weakest numbers in this study. Treat the 630–840 ceiling as
an order of magnitude, not a forecast.

## The geographic strategy the data actually supports

**Warehouse in Livonia or Madison Heights. Sell into Novi, Northville, Troy, Rochester Hills,
Farmington Hills and Canton.**

The reasoning:

- **Livonia has the cheapest verified industrial rent in the region ($8.20–$8.66/sq ft NNN)**
  and sits on I-96/I-275 with 30-minute reach to Novi, Northville, Plymouth, Canton, Farmington
  Hills and Southfield. Madison Heights trades slightly higher rent for I-75/I-696 access to
  Troy, Royal Oak, Rochester and Sterling Heights.
- **Your margin comes from the affluent western and northern suburbs, not from Detroit
  proper.** The Bosch B36CL81ENG in your lot is worth $2,200 in Novi and close to unsellable in
  Detroit. Every category in Part 5 realises a higher price and sells faster in the
  Novi–Troy–Rochester band.
- **Do not put a showroom in Detroit, Warren or Eastpointe.** Those markets are covered, and
  the price realisation is 15–20% lower on identical units.

## Adjacent demand worth cultivating

- **Property managers and multifamily operators** across Southfield, Warren, Dearborn and
  Detroit — steady 3- and 4-piece package demand, terrible margins (Part 13), excellent for
  clearing aged and heavy-cosmetic inventory that retail will not take.
- **Flippers** in Royal Oak, Ferndale, Redford, Hazel Park.
- **Delivery/installation subcontractors** are readily available in the region, which is why
  Part 8 recommends outsourcing delivery until you exceed ~250 units/month.
- **Appliance repair shops** are a *parts* channel: your salvage units are their inventory.
  Selling salvage to a repair shop beats scrapping it.
