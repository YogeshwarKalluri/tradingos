# PART 22 — INVESTMENT COMMITTEE DECISION

## The decision

# MODIFY

**Not BUY.** The 47-unit Lowe's lot at Shippensburg is a clear pass — my maximum bid of $4,500
is roughly half the market clearing price of $8,841–$11,515, and the bear case loses money at a
bid of zero.

**Not WALK AWAY entirely.** The core transaction genuinely works: 60.7% gross margin, 30%
contribution margin, 18.9% operating margin at one truck a month, break-even at 12 units. Very
few businesses available for $20,000 have those characteristics, and a real structural edge
exists in short-haul Michigan/Ohio sourcing that nobody appears to be exploiting systematically.

**MODIFY, on four specific terms:**

1. **Abandon the $1M-in-36-months target.** It is not in the reachable set — 0 of 4,000
   simulated paths. Replace it with **$250,000 of cumulative operating profit in 36 months and
   a business worth $300,000–$475,000 at the end.** That is roughly a 7.8% probability at the
   profit line and a genuinely achievable plan at the peak of the profit curve.
2. **Abandon out-of-state auction sourcing as the primary channel.** Build the Whirlpool /
   Ohio / regional-distributor / builder-overstock programme instead. This is worth $20–$25 per
   unit of freight — 16–20% of your entire contribution margin — and it attacks the assumption
   most likely to sink you.
3. **Target 199 units per month and stop.** That is where operating profit peaks at
   $10,620/month on ~$1.0M of revenue. **Do not open the second location.** Under base-case
   economics it destroys value, and the model refuses to open one because trailing profit never
   covers it.
4. **Raise the capital or shrink the entry.** $45,000–$60,000 is the honest requirement.
   If $20,000 is genuinely the limit, do not buy a truckload. Buy 8–12 individual units locally
   and learn your real sell-through and defect rates on $3,000 of risk.

## Probability of success

**Definition of "success":** the business is still operating at month 36, has produced positive
cumulative operating profit, and pays the owner a market-equivalent wage.

## Biggest risk

**Paying market price for inventory.**

The base case works at $165/unit landed. The observed market clearing price is $230–$290/unit
landed. At $191 — inside that range and below its midpoint — the business is insolvent by month
eight and the entire $20,000 is gone.

**The whole enterprise lives inside a 16% band of a single input, and that input is set by open
auction against bidders whose freight is shorter and whose labour is free.** No other risk in
Part 18 is close.

## Biggest competitive advantage

**Whirlpool's Michigan and Ohio footprint.**

Whirlpool is headquartered in Benton Harbor, Michigan, with plants across Ohio; GE is in
Louisville. A Detroit operator sourcing 150–250-mile loads pays **$13–$21 per unit of freight**
against **$31–$45** for a 480-mile Pennsylvania load.

That $20–$25 per unit is **16–20% of the entire contribution margin.** It is structural, it is
durable, it cannot be competed away by anyone outside Michigan and Ohio, and — critically — it
attacks the exact assumption most likely to kill you. **It is worth more than any operational
improvement available anywhere else in this business, and building the plan around it instead of
around B-Stock auctions is the single highest-value change you can make.**

## Conditions precedent, before any capital is committed

1. Michigan LLC, sales tax licence, Form 3372 resale certificate.
2. **EPA Section 608 Type I certification** — or a contracted certified recovery vendor with
   documentation retained.
3. **Attorney-drafted, conspicuous merchantability disclaimer** and a written 30-day limited
   warranty. Confirm in writing that your general liability policy does not exclude products
   liability.
4. Zoning confirmation **in writing** that retail customer traffic is permitted at the chosen
   Livonia or Madison Heights unit.
5. **Written maximum bid, calculated from your own comps, filed before each auction opens.**
6. The three-metric weekly dashboard from Part 18: landed cost per unit; units on hand ÷
   trailing-30-day sales; percentage of each load unsold at day 60.
7. **Test purchase first:** 8–12 individual units from local closeouts and builder overstock,
   fully processed and sold, before any truckload is bid on. Budget $3,000. If landed cost
   exceeds $180/unit or 30-day sell-through is under 38%, **stop and do not proceed.**

## Committee note

The analysis found a real business with real margins and a real edge. It did not find a
$1,000,000-in-36-months business, and no adjustment to the assumptions produced one that
survived contact with the observed market data.

The gap between what this is (a ~$250K-over-three-years owner-operated business with a
$300–475K terminal value and a 50% failure rate) and what was proposed (a $1M-profit business)
is not a matter of execution intensity. **It is arithmetic: $27,800 per month required against a
$10,620 per month ceiling.**

**Recommendation stands: MODIFY. Pass on the current lot. Rebuild the sourcing strategy around
short-haul Michigan/Ohio programmes. Target the peak of the profit curve and stay there.**
