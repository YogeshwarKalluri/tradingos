## What this table tells you

**1. Over-the-range microwaves lose money.** Every unit absorbs the same 1.65 hours of labour
regardless of its price, so a $140 sale cannot carry $91 of fixed cost after $50 of processing.
**Do not buy lots heavy in microwaves.** If a lot contains them, value them at zero and take
whatever the salvage/parts channel gives you, or bundle them free with a range to close a sale.
The same logic makes dishwashers marginal at $98 net over 52 days.

**2. Refrigerators are the business.** Mid-range french-door units return $396 net over 45
days — $8.80 per day of tied-up capital, more than double a top-load washer. **Deliberately
skew your buying toward refrigeration and front-load laundry, and away from small-ticket
boxes.** This single decision moves your average net per unit more than any negotiation will.

**3. The premium unit is the highest-return and highest-risk line simultaneously.** $1,343 net
is spectacular; 110 days to sell is not. On a per-day-of-capital basis it still wins ($12.21),
but only if it sells at all — and it only sells in Novi, Northville, Troy or Rochester Hills.
**One or two premium units per truck is an edge. Five is a liquidity trap.**

**4. The $91 of fixed-cost absorption is the whole argument of this study.** It is 1.65 hours
of labour at $4,600/FTE-month plus rent. It applies identically to a $140 microwave and a
$2,200 Bosch. **Every unit you handle must clear $91 before it earns anything**, which is why
average ticket matters far more than volume, and why the business peaks at 199 units/month
(Part 11) rather than scaling indefinitely.

**5. Ancillary revenue is thinner than it looks.** Delivery, install, haul-away and warranty
add $74–$111 of revenue per unit but cost $44–$56 to deliver. **Net ancillary contribution is
roughly $20–$55 per unit** — real, but not the profit centre the industry markets it as. See
Part 8.

## Sensitivity: what a $25 change in average sale price does

At 200 units/month, a **$25** move in average selling price — 6% — is **$5,000/month, $60,000/year,
and it is roughly 50% of base-case operating profit.** A $25 move in average landed cost is the
same magnitude in the opposite direction.

**Pricing discipline and buying discipline are each worth more than doubling your volume.**
