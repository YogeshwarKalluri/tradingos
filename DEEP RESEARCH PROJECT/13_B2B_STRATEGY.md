# PART 13 — B2B STRATEGY

## The package economics

| | **Single unit (retail)** | **3-piece package** | **4-piece package** | **Whole-house (5-piece)** |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Typical composition | 1 appliance | range + fridge + dishwasher | + OTR microwave | + washer & dryer |
| Retail value of the same units | $358 | $1,075 | $1,215 | $1,910 |
| **B2B package price** | — | **$895** | **$995** | **$1,550** |
| Discount to retail | — | 17% | 18% | 19% |
| Landed cost | $165 | $495 | $525 | $825 |
| **Gross profit** | **$193** | **$400** | **$470** | **$725** |

| **Contribution per unit per day** | **$2.29** | **$3.25** | **$2.71** | **$3.67** |

## Read that table carefully

**B2B cuts contribution per unit by roughly 60–70% — from $126 to $33–$55.** That is the cost
of the discount plus the fact that a package is one negotiation for three or four units of
merchandise, so you cannot charge retail prices.

**But it cuts days-to-sell by 75% — from 55 days to 12–15.** On a *return-on-capital-per-day*
basis, B2B beats retail: **$3.25–$3.67 per unit per day versus $2.29.**

So the honest answer to your question — *does B2B provide faster turnover even at lower
margins?* — is **yes, and it improves return on capital, but it lowers absolute profit per unit
and it will not, on its own, get you to $1M.** If you replaced your entire book with B2B at
$45/unit contribution, the base case's 199-unit peak would produce **$8,955/month of
contribution against $8,000+ of fixed cost** — barely break-even. **B2B cannot be the primary
channel. It is the release valve.**

## Where B2B genuinely earns its place

**1. It clears the inventory retail will not take.** Part 5 shows 25% of every truck arrives
with heavy cosmetic damage and Part 11 shows 14% is still sitting at day 90. A landlord
furnishing a rental unit does not care about a dented side panel that faces a wall. **Sell your
clean units at retail and your damaged and aged units into B2B.** This is the single highest-
value use of the channel: it converts your worst inventory from a 7% salvage write-off into
$33–$55 of contribution per unit.

**2. It smooths seasonality.** Retail appliance demand peaks in spring (March–May) and around
Memorial Day, Labor Day and Black Friday, and troughs in January–February. **Landlord turnover
demand is counter-cyclical to none of that, but it is far less spiky** — leases turn all year.
A B2B book is how you keep the warehouse moving in February.

**3. It amortises delivery.** Retail delivery averages ~1.3 units per stop. A 4-piece package
is 4 units on one stop. The same $48–$68 of delivery cost carries three times the merchandise —
which is why the delivery line in the table above barely rises as the package grows.

**4. It is repeat revenue with near-zero CAC.** A property manager with 200 doors replaces
appliances continuously. Winning one is worth more than a hundred Marketplace leads, and costs
relationship time rather than money.

## The Metro Detroit B2B map

| Segment | Where | Volume potential | Price sensitivity | Verdict |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Property managers / multifamily** | Southfield, Warren, Dearborn, Detroit, Ypsilanti | **Highest** | Extreme — they will grind you | **Primary target.** Aim for 3–5 relationships, not 30. |
| **Landlords (small, 2–20 doors)** | Detroit, Redford, Hazel Park, Eastpointe | High | High | Excellent. Reachable on Marketplace and Nextdoor. |
| **Home flippers** | Royal Oak, Ferndale, Redford, Hazel Park, Detroit | Medium | Medium — they want *matching* finishes | **Best margin in B2B.** They will pay up for a matched stainless set. |
| **Student rental operators** | Ann Arbor, Ypsilanti | Medium, seasonal (May–August) | High | Good, but needs its own delivery route |
| **Builders** | Novi, Northville, Canton, Brighton | Low for S&D | Low | Poor fit — they buy new through distributors on trade terms you cannot match |
| **Real estate investors / BRRRR** | Metro-wide | Medium | High | Good; overlaps with landlords |

## Recommended B2B policy

- **Cap B2B at 30–35% of units.** Above that, blended contribution falls below the level that
  covers fixed cost.
- **Route damaged, aged (60+ days) and mismatched inventory to B2B first.** Never discount a
  clean, current unit into a package when it would sell at retail in 40 days.
- **Price packages off a floor, not off retail.** Set a hard minimum contribution of $35/unit
  and walk away below it. Property managers will test this on every order.
- **Match finishes.** Flippers pay a genuine premium for a matched stainless set and it costs
  you nothing but sorting discipline.
- **Terms: cash or card on delivery for the first three orders. Net-15 only after that, and
  only with a signed credit application.** Extending net-30 to a property management company
  while you are working on $20,000 of capital is how a profitable business runs out of money.
