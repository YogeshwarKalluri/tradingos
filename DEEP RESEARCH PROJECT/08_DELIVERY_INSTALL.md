**The competitor benchmarks are the binding constraint, not the national price guides.** AOK
gives delivery away above a $300 ticket. If your average ticket is $358 and a nearby
competitor delivers free, **you cannot reliably charge $89 in Eastpointe or Warren.** You can
in Novi and Rochester Hills, which is another reason Part 3 points the showroom west and north.

## What I model, and why it is conservative

| | Attach rate | Price | Direct cost | **Net per attached job** |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Delivery | 45% | $89 | $48 | **+$41** |
| Installation | 25% | $79 | $28 | **+$51** |
| Haul-away | 20% | $40 | $20 | **+$20** |
| Protection plan | 15% | $129 | $65 | **+$64** |
| **Blended, per unit sold** | | **$62 revenue** | **$42 cost** | **≈ +$20** |

**Delivery and installation are close to a wash.** They exist to close the sale, not to make
money. A customer who cannot get the unit home does not buy it; that is the actual return on
offering delivery, and it is large. But do not model it as a profit centre — the vendors who
sell you delivery software will, and they are wrong.

## The four models compared

Modelled at **200 units/month**, 45% delivery attach = **90 deliveries/month**.

| Model | Fixed cost/mo | Variable cost/delivery | **Total cost at 90 deliveries** | **Net contribution** | Risk profile |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---|
| **A. Outsourced** (3rd-party appliance delivery contractor) | $0 | $48 | **$4,320** | **+$3,690** | Lowest. No trucks, no WC exposure on drivers, no commercial auto. Scheduling control is weak; damage disputes are with a third party. |
| **B. Employee crew** (2 people + box truck) | $8,435 payroll + $1,050 truck/insurance/fuel = **$9,485** | $12 | **$10,565** | **−$2,555** | Highest fixed cost. Needs ~210 deliveries/month to beat outsourcing. Commercial auto $1,800–$3,500/vehicle/yr; workers' comp mandatory from employee one in Michigan. |
| **C. Owner-operated** (owner + one helper) | $3,776 helper + $1,050 truck = **$4,826** | $12 | **$5,906** | **+$2,104** | Cheapest in cash but consumes 40–60 owner-hours/month that are worth more on buying and pricing (Part 7: a $25 pricing move is worth $5,000/month). |
| **D. Hybrid** — outsource baseline, own truck for premium/scheduled/B2B jobs | $1,050 truck | $48 outsourced / $12 own (≈30% own) | **$4,272** | **+$3,738** | **Best.** Keeps the flexibility and the margin, controls the jobs where damage or timing actually matters. |

### Profit difference, ranked at 200 units/month

1. **Hybrid (D): +$3,738/month**
2. Outsourced (A): +$3,690/month
3. Owner-operated (C): +$2,104/month *(and it costs you the owner's most valuable hours)*
4. Employee crew (B): **−$2,555/month**

**Difference between best and worst: $6,293/month, or $75,500/year** — larger than the entire
base-case Year 1 operating profit. This is not a minor operational choice.

## Recommendation

**Start fully outsourced. Move to hybrid at roughly 150 units/month. Do not bring delivery
fully in-house below ~250 units/month (≈115 deliveries), and only then if you have already
proved you can keep a crew busy.**

The crossover is at **~210 deliveries/month**, i.e. ~470 units/month at a 45% attach rate.
Below that, an employed crew is idle capacity you are paying full price for, and my model
(Part 11) says you will probably never get there.

Two Michigan-specific cautions:

- **Workers' compensation is mandatory from the first employee in Michigan — there is no
  small-team exemption.** Hiring a delivery helper is not a marginal decision; it triggers a
  compliance regime.
- **Commercial auto runs $1,800–$3,500 per vehicle per year** for work-truck coverage. Budget
  it before you buy the truck, not after.

## The one place delivery genuinely makes money

**B2B.** A property manager taking a 4-piece package to one address is a single delivery
carrying four units of contribution. Retail delivery averages 1.3 units per stop; B2B packages
average 3.5. The same $48 of cost is spread across nearly three times as much merchandise.
See Part 13.
