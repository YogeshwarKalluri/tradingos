Purchasing in every month is the minimum of three constraints — **cash available**, **sourcing
supply at a disciplined price**, and **forward demand cover** (never more than 2 months of
expected sales on hand).

Profit is shown **before owner compensation**. Owner salary is a separate line and begins in
month 13. The owner works the floor as 1.5 FTE in year one and 1.0 FTE thereafter.

**What the base case actually is:** by month 24 it hits the single-location ceiling of ~208
units/month and stays there. Year 3 is a **$1.05M-revenue business earning $121,194 of
operating profit**, from which the owner draws $54,000, leaving **$67,194 retained**. Total
owner economics over 36 months: **$162,000 of salary plus $129,001 of retained profit =
$291,001**, on $20,000 of capital and three years of full-time work.

That is roughly **$97,000 per year of total owner economics.** It is a real, decent outcome. It
is not $1,000,000.

Note the second location never opens: the model only opens one when trailing three-month
profit covers 1.5× the new site's fixed cost, and in the base case it never does.

Part 15 quantifies how likely that conjunction is. The short answer is: it did not occur once
in 4,000 simulated paths.

## Reading the cash column

The base case never goes below **$9,530 of cash**, which looks safe — but that is a
*consequence* of the model's discipline, not a property of the business. The model refuses to
buy inventory that would take cash below 90% of one month's fixed cost. **A real operator
looking at a good-looking auction will break that rule, and that is the single most common
cause of death in this industry.** Part 18 scores it.
