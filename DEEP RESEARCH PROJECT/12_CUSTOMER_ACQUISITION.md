search. Appliance *retail* has lower intent per click and a higher ticket. Treat $30–$70 as the
realistic paid-search cost per **lead**, and apply a close rate.

## What the numbers imply

**Your model budgets $8.50 of marketing per unit purchased — about $1,700/month at 200
units/month.** That is deliberately small, and it is only achievable because the top three
channels are effectively free.

Do the arithmetic on paid search: at a $10.28 CPC, a 6% click-to-lead rate and a 45%
lead-to-sale rate, a sale costs **$381 in ad spend.** Contribution per unit is $126.50.
**Google Ads loses money on your average unit and only works on refrigerators over $700 and
premium units.** Run it narrowly or not at all.

## The recommended stack, in order

1. **Facebook Marketplace, run properly.** Every unit listed within 24 hours of being
   photographed, relisted every 7 days, priced to the local floor, responded to within 15
   minutes. This is 14 minutes per unit in the labour standard and it is where most of your
   volume comes from. There is no substitute and no shortcut.
2. **Google Business Profile with aggressive review collection.** Free, compounding, and the
   thing that eventually lets you charge more than the Marketplace floor. **Ask every single
   customer at delivery.** Note the cautionary example in Part 2: a well-known Metro Detroit
   used-appliance dealer carries a **BBB rating of F with two unanswered complaints.** In this
   business reputation is the only durable pricing power, and it is destroyed by ignoring
   complaints.
3. **OfferUp and Craigslist** as no-cost secondary listings.
4. **B2B relationships** from month 3 (Part 13) — property managers, then flippers.
5. **Local SEO** from month 6 — city-plus-category pages for Livonia, Novi, Canton, Westland,
   Farmington Hills.
6. **Nextdoor** in the affluent suburbs where your premium units actually sell.
7. **Google LSA / search ads** only for refrigeration and premium units, only once your close
   rate is measured.
8. **Never** billboards, radio or direct mail at this scale.

## The uncomfortable truth about free channels

Facebook Marketplace is free, which means **it is free for your competitors too, and for the
private seller offloading a working dryer for $200.** Verified Detroit listings: LG gas dryer
$345, LG washer $250, Frigidaire refrigerator $200. Your customer sees your $385 scratch-and-
dent gas dryer next to a $345 used one on the same screen.

**Your entire pricing premium rests on three things**: the unit is *new* rather than used, you
offer a warranty, and you deliver and install it. If you cannot communicate all three in a
Marketplace listing, you are competing on price against people with no cost structure at all —
and you will lose, because their cost basis is zero.
