# PART 1 — INDUSTRY RESEARCH

## 1. Market size

| Metric | Figure | Source quality |
|---|---|---|
| US scratch-and-dent appliance market | **$9.205B (2024)**, forecast $18.314B by 2035 | Trade source, single origin — treat as **directional only** |
| US reverse-logistics market | **$24.6B (2025) → $48.9B (2031)** | Mobility Foresights |
| Global reverse logistics | $711B (2025, IMARC) to $872B (2025, GMI) | Estimates disagree by 23% |

**Caution.** The $9.2B scratch-and-dent figure appears in vendor marketing and I could not
trace it to a primary research house. Two other reverse-logistics estimates for the same year
differ by 23%. **Do not build a plan on any of these numbers.** The number that actually
governs your business is local: Metro Detroit absorbable volume (Part 3), which I estimate
bottom-up at ~10,500 secondary-market appliance units per month across all competitors.

## 2. Growth rate

6.45%/yr claimed for scratch-and-dent; 12.6% CAGR for US reverse logistics. Both plausible
directionally — e-commerce returns are rising and appliances are bulky and expensive to
restock. **Neither growth rate helps you.** Growth in liquidation *supply* increases the
number of loads available but also increases the number of bidders; growth in *demand* is a
consumer-affordability story that pushes buyers toward the used market, which is your
substitute as much as your customer.

**Sources (sellers):** Lowe's, Home Depot, Costco, Best Buy, GE Appliances, Whirlpool,
Samsung, LG, Frigidaire/Electrolux, Bosch — plus regional distributors, builder overstock,
and insurance salvage.

**Marketplaces / liquidators (buyers' side):**

| Platform | Model | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| **B-Stock** | Auction marketplace hosting each retailer's own private storefront — Costco, Walmart, Target, Lowe's, Home Depot, Best Buy, GE, Whirlpool and 40+ others | The dominant channel. 10% buyer's premium. Resale certificate required, approval typically within one business day. |
| Liquidation.com | Auction, mixed manifested/unmanifested | Lowe's DC pallets and truckloads |
| Direct Liquidation | Auction + fixed price | Carries Lowe's major-appliance customer-return truckloads (e.g. 60 pcs) |
| NeuAppliance Wholesale | Fixed-price truckload programs (Lowe's, Best Buy, Frigidaire, LG) | Pre-built manifests; no auction |
| Select Liquidation, Box Flipz, Via Trading, Department Store Liquidations | Fixed-price truckloads | Middlemen — they bought at auction and mark up |
| K & A Appliance (Lancaster/Willow Street PA) | Regional wholesaler | Sells to independent S&D retailers |

**Structural point:** the fixed-price truckload sellers are re-sellers of auction inventory.
Buying from them means paying someone else's margin on top of the auction price you already
cannot afford. They exist because they take the sorting risk — which is real — but their
pricing is above the auction clearing price, which is already above my calculated maximum.

## 5–7. Truckload sizes, acquisition prices, price as % of MSRP

**Verified B-Stock Lowe's scratch-and-dent appliance lot data:**

| Lot | Units | Condition | Extended retail | Avg cost/unit | Retail/unit | **Cost as % of "retail"** |
|---|---:|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Dryers, refrigerators, dishwashers, microwaves (Samsung, GE) | 50 | Scratch & Dent | $45,284 | **$245.00** | $906 | **27.0%** |
| Dryers, washer/dryers (Whirlpool, Samsung, LG) | 63 | Used | $83,557 | **$188.10** | $1,326 | **14.2%** |
| Dryers, ranges, refrigerators, washers (Whirlpool, Haier) | 53 | Scratch & Dent | $63,436 | **$189.62** | $1,197 | **15.8%** |

**Typical truckload:** 39–63 major appliances; a 53' trailer holds 22–24 tall pallets, or is
floor-loaded. Your lot (47 units, floor-loaded) is normal.

**The number that matters: $188–$245 per unit is the observed clearing price.** For a 47-unit
lot that is **$8,841–$11,515** before the 10% buyer's premium and before freight — i.e.
**$230–$290 per unit landed** in Metro Detroit. Part 6 shows why that is roughly double what
the lot is worth to a Detroit operator.

**Do not read "% of MSRP" as a margin.** These lots clear at 14–27% of *extended retail*, and
extended retail is itself ~25% above street price (Part 5). The real ratio is **cost ≈ 18–34%
of street price**, and you resell at ~50–55% of street. That is the whole business.

## 13–15. Defect, return and repair rates

No liquidator publishes defect rates, and B-Stock's own condition guidance is explicit that a
"scratch & dent" lot can contain anything from a colour-preference return to a non-functioning
unit. My condition distribution for a **floor-loaded, warehouse-damaged** lot:

| Condition | Bear | **Base** | Bull | Consequence |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---|
| Clean cosmetic — sells at full S&D price | 42% | **55%** | 66% | — |
| Heavy cosmetic damage | 28% | **25%** | 22% | sells at 70% of S&D price |
| Functional fault, economic to repair | 16% | **12%** | 8% | $150 avg repair, sells at 80% |
| Salvage / parts only | 14% | **8%** | 4% | ~$60 recovery, disposal cost |

**Grade drives this more than the label does.** Wholesaler testing reports claim genuine
retailer S&D loads run ~95% "no repairs needed / 5% minor repairs", while customer-return
loads can be ~30% ready-to-sell with ~50% needing a part. That 95% is about **function, not
cosmetics** — a true S&D load has *more* cosmetic damage than a customer-return load and
*fewer* functional failures, so a better grade moves units out of repairable/salvage and into
heavy-cosmetic, not into clean. Full grade ladder and what each is worth in bid terms:
Part 5 Step 3b and Part 6.

**[ESTIMATE]** — derived, not sourced. Floor-loading matters: units are hand-stacked loose
rather than palletised, so transit damage is materially higher than for a palletised load.

**Returns:** 6% reserve on merchandise revenue (base). Michigan's implied warranty of
merchantability applies unless conspicuously disclaimed in writing using the word
"merchantability" — see Part 17. A refrigerator that does not keep food cold is a breach
regardless of what your receipt says about "as-is", unless the disclaimer is properly drafted.

**Repair economics:** Michigan-area labour $85–$135/hr. Control board $320–$520 installed;
compressor $700–$1,250 (sealed system, welding, recharge); washer repairs $175–$350;
transmission >$500. **A sealed-system refrigerator fault is almost never economic** on a unit
you will sell for $600. Scrap it and take the $60.

## 16. Freight

Shippensburg PA → Metro Detroit ≈ **480 miles**. Mid-2026 dry-van spot: $2.05–$2.80/mile
linehaul, **$2.54–$3.59/mile all-in** with fuel surcharge (diesel $5.26/gal, FSC 20–35% of
linehaul). A 500-mile FTL runs **$1,000–$1,400**; floor-loaded appliance freight sits at the
top of that band because of hand-unload and damage exposure.

**Model input: $1,450 (bull) / $1,750 (base) / $2,100 (bear) = $31–$45 per unit.** On a
$126.50 contribution margin, freight alone is 25–35% of contribution. **This is why a
480-mile load is structurally worse than a 200-mile load, and why Whirlpool's Michigan/Ohio
footprint is the single best sourcing asset available to a Detroit operator.**

## 19. Delivery / installation revenue

Michigan and national 2026 rates: appliance delivery **$89–$179**; dishwasher install
**$110–$270** (replacement with existing connections $200–$500); range/oven install
**$200–$225** basic; Pontiac MI dishwasher service averages **$156** (range $106–$205).

Model (base): delivery $89 revenue / $48 cost at 45% attach; install $79/$28 at 25%;
haul-away $40/$20 at 20%. **Net ancillary contribution ≈ $20 per unit sold** — delivery and
install are close to a wash unless they absorb otherwise-idle labour. See Part 8.

## 20. Warranty / protection-plan economics

Third-party administrators pay retailers **25–50% of plan revenue**; provider gross margins
run 40–60%. Trade sources claim protection plans add **10–25% more gross profit per sale**,
and that attach rates rise 20–40% after provider onboarding; one vendor claims a 12–28%
revenue uplift on scratch-and-dent specifically.

**Treat those uplift claims with suspicion — they come from warranty vendors.** My model uses
a **15% attach rate at $129 with ~50% retained**, i.e. **≈$10 per unit sold**. That is real
money at scale (about 8% of contribution) but it is not a business model, and it carries a
liability tail if you self-administer. **Use a third-party administrator; never self-insure.**
