## The honest ranking

**For reaching $1,000,000 of cumulative profit in 36 months from $20,000, every option here is
a long shot, and the appliance-liquidation truckload model is close to the worst of them.**
Not because appliances are a bad business — because the model combines the two worst
properties for rapid compounding:

- **Inventory-heavy**, so every dollar of growth is locked up for 55 days before it returns, and
- **Fixed-cost-heavy at scale**, so the growth that does happen has a declining marginal return.

Businesses that compound to $1M in three years from $20,000 are almost always **labour- or
software-leveraged**, not inventory-leveraged. That is why line 11 scores highest even though it
is the vaguest.

## The three genuinely better uses of $20,000 in this same industry

**1. Appliance repair (line 6).** Verified Google Ads cost per lead of $29–$51 and LSA at
$33.11 median. Near-zero inventory. Gross margins on parts plus labour of 60–70%. **No
truckloads, no warehouse, no freight, no dead stock, no auction against better-positioned
bidders.** $20,000 buys a van, tools, certifications (including EPA 608) and six months of
marketing. The ceiling is your own hours — but the ceiling on the appliance-liquidation model
turns out to be $10,620/month anyway, and repair gets there with a fraction of the capital risk
and none of the inventory exposure.

**2. Delivery and installation contracting (line 7).** You would be the outsourced partner every
operator in Part 8 should be using. Contract revenue, B2B customers, no inventory, and you learn
the entire local appliance ecosystem — including which liquidators are actually profitable —
from the inside. **This is also the cheapest possible reconnaissance on the business you
actually want to build.**

**3. Used appliance dealership at deliberate small scale (line 5).** Best cash conversion in the
table. Buy locally, one to five units at a time, from estate sales, closeouts, builder overstock
and Marketplace itself. **No freight, no auction, no truckload risk, and you can inspect every
unit before you pay for it** — which eliminates failure modes 1, 4, 6 and 17 from Part 18
outright. This is the version of your idea that actually works, and it is how I would spend the
$20,000 if you are determined to be in appliances.
