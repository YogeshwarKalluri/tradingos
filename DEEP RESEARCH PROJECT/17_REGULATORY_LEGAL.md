# PART 17 — MICHIGAN REGULATORY AND LEGAL

**This is a summary of research, not legal advice. Have a Michigan business attorney review
your sale documents and your warranty language before your first sale — the total cost is
perhaps $1,500 and it addresses the single largest uninsured liability in this business.**

## Registration and tax

| Requirement | Detail | Cost |
|---|---|---|
| **Entity** | LLC registered with Michigan LARA | **$50** |
| **Sales tax licence** | Register via **MTO eRegistration** or mail **Form 518**. Michigan sales tax **6%** on retail sales of tangible personal property, including used and scratch-and-dent appliances. | Free |
| **Resale certificate** | **Form 3372** with "Resale at Retail" checked plus your Michigan Sales Tax Licence number. Michigan does **not** require you to register the certificate with the state — you present it to sellers. | Free |
| **Blanket certificate validity** | **Expires every 4 years**, and earlier if there is no "recurring business relationship" (no purchase within 12 months). **Diary this — an expired certificate can invalidate your B-Stock account.** | — |
| **Misuse warning** | Using the resale certificate for anything you are not reselling (office supplies, personal purchases) is unlawful. | — |
| **Federal** | EIN; Schedule C or 1120-S; quarterly estimated tax | Free |
| **Local** | City business registration, zoning/certificate of occupancy for retail use, sign permit | Varies |

**Zoning is the trap.** An industrial-zoned Livonia unit may not permit retail customer traffic.
**Confirm retail use is allowed in writing before signing a lease** — this is the most common
avoidable regulatory problem in this business.

## Appliance-dealer-specific requirements

**Michigan does not impose a specialised "used appliance dealer" licence.** You are a retailer
of tangible personal property. The real regulatory exposure is in three places: **warranty
law, refrigerants, and gas/electrical work.**

### 1. Warranty and consumer protection — your largest liability

Two statutes govern:

- **MCL 440.2314 (UCC 2-314)** — an **implied warranty of merchantability** arises in every
  sale by a merchant of goods of that kind. In plain terms: *if you are in the business of
  selling refrigerators, every sale carries an implied promise that the refrigerator will keep
  food cold.*
- **MCL 440.2316 (UCC 2-316)** — to exclude it, the language **must mention the word
  "merchantability" and must be conspicuous**. "Sold as is" or "with all faults" is the
  standard formula, but **without the word "merchantability", conspicuously displayed, the
  disclaimer fails and the implied warranty survives.**
- **Michigan Consumer Protection Act, MCL 445.903** — it is an unlawful practice to disclaim
  or limit the implied warranty of merchantability **unless the disclaimer is clearly and
  conspicuously disclosed**. So the MCPA independently penalises a buried disclaimer.

**What this means operationally:**

- A one-line "as-is" on a receipt is **not** sufficient. You need a **separate, conspicuous,
  signed disclaimer** using the word "merchantability", presented **before** payment.
- **Do not verbally promise that a unit "works fine" and then sell it "as-is".** That is an
  express warranty (MCL 440.2313) which an as-is clause does not defeat, and it is precisely
  how these disputes arise.
- **Offering a 30- or 60-day warranty is commercially wise and legally safer than a bare
  as-is sale.** Both Michigan competitors I verified do this — AOK Appliance offers a 30-day
  warranty, Appliance Mart 60 days. My model reserves 6% of merchandise revenue for returns
  and warranty, which is roughly one full unit per truckload. **Budget for it; do not pretend
  it away.**
- Federal **Magnuson-Moss** applies to any written warranty you do give: it must be designated
  "Full" or "Limited" and available for pre-sale review.

### 2. Refrigerants — EPA Section 608

**This is a genuine criminal-and-civil exposure that new operators routinely overlook.**

- **EPA Section 608** (Clean Air Act) makes it unlawful to knowingly vent ozone-depleting
  refrigerants or their substitutes. **Refrigerant must be recovered by a certified technician
  using certified equipment before a refrigerator, freezer or air conditioner is scrapped or
  disposed of.**
- **This applies to your salvage units.** In the base case, ~8% of every truckload is
  salvage-only, and a share of those are refrigeration. Sending a refrigerator to a scrapper
  without documented recovery is a violation.
- **Section 608 Type I certification** (small appliances) is inexpensive and takes a day. **Get
  it, or contract exclusively with a certified recovery vendor and keep the documentation.**
- Any repair opening a sealed system requires certification. Part 1 shows sealed-system repairs
  are rarely economic anyway — but the *disposal* obligation applies regardless.
- Recovered refrigerant and scrapped units also fall under **Michigan EGLE** solid/hazardous
  waste rules and local disposal ordinances.

### 3. Gas and electrical work

- **Gas appliance connection in Michigan is licensed mechanical work.** A dryer or range gas
  hookup performed by an unlicensed installer is both a code violation and an uninsurable
  liability. **Subcontract every gas connection to a licensed mechanical contractor**, and
  verify their licence and insurance certificate annually.
- **Hard-wired electrical work** (a 240V range circuit, a new dryer receptacle) is licensed
  electrical work. Plugging an appliance into an existing receptacle is not.
- **Your in-warehouse gas and 240V testing installation must itself be permitted and installed
  by licensed trades** (Part 9). Do not improvise it.
- **Practical policy: deliver and connect cord-and-plug electrical appliances; subcontract
  every gas connection and every hard-wired installation.** Say so explicitly in your
  advertising so customers do not expect otherwise.

## The five things that could actually hurt you

1. **A house fire or flood traced to an appliance you sold or connected.** This is the
   catastrophic tail. A dryer you sold that was improperly vented, a washer hose that failed, a
   gas range connected by an unlicensed helper. **Mitigation: subcontract gas and hard-wired
   work to licensed insured trades, confirm products liability coverage in writing, document
   every function test with photographs and a signed condition report.**
2. **A defective disclaimer.** Sell 200 units a year on an "as-is" that does not say
   "merchantability" conspicuously, and every one of those sales carries an implied warranty
   you thought you had excluded. **Mitigation: attorney-drafted, separately signed disclaimer;
   offer a short express warranty instead of relying on a bare as-is.**
3. **Venting refrigerant.** Strict-liability federal exposure on units you were going to throw
   away anyway. **Mitigation: Section 608 Type I certification, or a certified vendor with
   documentation retained.**
4. **An uninsured injury.** Appliances are heavy, warehouses have forklifts, and Michigan
   requires workers' comp from employee one. **Mitigation: comply from the first hire; do not
   use "1099 helpers" to avoid it — misclassification is its own liability.**
5. **A Michigan Consumer Protection Act claim or an unanswered BBB complaint pattern.** Part 2
   found a Metro Detroit used-appliance dealer carrying a **BBB rating of F with two unanswered
   complaints.** In a business whose only pricing power is reputation, that is a slow-motion
   business-ending event. **Mitigation: answer every complaint, honour the warranty you wrote,
   and treat refunds as a marketing cost.**
