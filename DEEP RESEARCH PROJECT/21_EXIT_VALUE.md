# PART 21 — EXIT VALUE

## Comparable multiples (verified)

| Basis | Multiple | Source |
|---|---|---|
| **Electronics & appliance stores — revenue** | **0.3× – 0.6×** | DealStream industry rules of thumb |
| **Electronics & appliance stores — EBITDA** | **3× – 5×** | DealStream |
| **Electronics & appliance stores — SDE** | **2× – 3×** | DealStream |
| Inventory discount applied at sale | **10% – 40%** | DealStream |
| Value per square foot | $50 – $150 | DealStream |
| **All US small-business sales, 2025** | **2.61× SDE, 0.69× revenue** across 9,586 closed transactions | BizBuySell 2025 Insight Report |

**SDE (seller's discretionary earnings) is the right basis below roughly $500K of earnings** —
it adds back the owner's salary. **EBITDA is the right basis above that**, where a buyer must
hire a manager. Confusing the two is the most common way sellers overvalue businesses like this.

Cross-check against the revenue multiple: 0.3–0.6× on $1M is $300K–$600K, and BizBuySell's
0.69× average gives $690K. **The 0.69× economy-wide average is too generous for this sector** —
inventory-heavy, low-margin, owner-dependent local retail sits at the bottom of the range. I
would underwrite **0.3–0.4× revenue.**

## The four things that would cut the multiple

**1. Owner dependence.** If you are the buyer, the pricer, the closer and the face of the
business, a buyer is purchasing your job. **That is the difference between 2× SDE and 3×** —
about $175,000 on a $1M-revenue business. Documenting the process and installing a manager is
worth more than a year of growth.

**2. Inventory quality.** The comparables apply a **10–40% discount to inventory at sale**. Aged
and dead stock is discounted hardest. Your Part 11 dead-stock tail is not just a margin problem
— **it is a permanent haircut on your exit price.**

**3. Supplier concentration.** A business dependent on winning B-Stock auctions has no
transferable supply. **A business with negotiated Whirlpool/Ohio programme relationships has a
transferable asset**, and that difference shows up directly in the multiple. This is a fifth
argument for the sourcing strategy in Part 4.

**4. Reputation.** Google rating and BBB standing are diligence items. The verified Metro Detroit
competitor carrying a **BBB F with unanswered complaints** has materially impaired its own exit
value, probably without realising it.

## What the exit is actually worth relative to the effort

Base case, month 36: business worth **$300,000–$475,000**, having produced **$129,001 of
retained profit** and paid you **$162,000 of salary**.

**Total three-year economics: roughly $600,000–$766,000 of combined salary, retained profit and
equity value, on $20,000 of capital and three years of full-time work.**

That is a genuinely good outcome — a 30–38× return on invested capital. **It is also not
$1,000,000 of cumulative profit, and the two should not be confused.** Equity value is not
profit; you only realise it if you find a buyer, and the buyer pool for a $400,000 owner-operated
appliance business in Metro Detroit is thin.
